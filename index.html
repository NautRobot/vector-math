1. The Definition of a Limit

The limit of a function f(x) as x approaches c is the value that f(x) gets closer to as x gets closer to c. Understanding this behavior is fundamental before proceeding to derivatives.

lim (x -> c) f(x) = L

Remember to check both left-hand and right-hand limits. If they do not equate, the general limit does not exist at that specific point.

2. Derivatives and the Power Rule

A derivative represents the instantaneous rate of change of a function. The most common operational theorem you will use is the Power Rule. When faced with a polynomial, this rule simplifies the differentiation process immensely.

d/dx [x^n] = n * x^(n-1)

Do not forget to apply the constant multiple rule alongside the power rule. For example, d/dx [5x^3] = 15x^2.

3. The Chain Rule

For composite functions, the chain rule is mandatory. You must differentiate the outer function leaving the inner function untouched, and then multiply by the derivative of the inner function.

d/dx [f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) * g'(x)

4. Introduction to Integrals

Integration is essentially the reverse process of differentiation. It is used to find areas under curves. Remember to always include the constant of integration, C, when performing indefinite integrals.

∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C (where F'(x) = f(x))
